Privacy

NUMBER//ZERO is a hobby game. All credits are virtual and have no cash value.

Google sign-in and online play

When you sign in, the server verifies a Google ID token and stores your Google account identifier and display name. It also stores your online credit balance, daily-claim date, battle participation, results, and account statistics. The application does not store your Google password, email address, or profile photo, and does not request access to Gmail, Drive, or other Google account content.

Your display name, game account ID, battle participation, and battle results are visible to other players through public lobbies and battle links. Your Google account identifier and session credentials are not included in public battle responses.

Browser storage and sessions

Practice progress, inventory, custom cases, favorites, and preferences are stored in your browser's local storage. An online session token is kept in session storage for the current tab. The server stores a hash of that token; sessions expire after seven days and are revoked when you sign out. Clearing browser storage removes local progress but does not delete your online account.

Hosting and service providers

Google processes sign-in requests. Render hosts the application and API, Turso stores online game data, and GitHub Pages hosts an alternative frontend. These providers may process technical request information, such as IP addresses and browser details, under their own privacy policies. The application uses request addresses for rate limiting and may log server errors. Google account information is used to authenticate players and operate the game, not sold or used for advertising.

Retention and deletion requests

Online accounts and battle records do not currently have an automatic deletion schedule. To request access, correction, or deletion of your online data, contact daminion.minion@gmail.com. Include your display name or game account ID, but never send a password or session token. Account ownership may need to be verified before a request can be fulfilled.

Local progress can be removed using your browser's site-data controls. Provider backups may retain data for their configured retention periods.
